# Practise Asking Better Questions

[← Case collection](README.md)

## Before a meeting

Choose the decision you need to support and the stakeholder who can clarify it. Select three to eight questions, not the entire bank. Identify what evidence would change your recommendation and which specialist decisions are outside your authority.

## During discovery

Begin with purpose and the current situation. Ask for a real example. Explore the step or rule implicated by that example. Probe exceptions, impact, and competing explanations. Paraphrase what you heard, then confirm it. Adapt this sequence to the conversation.

| If an answer is… | A useful next move |
| --- | --- |
| Vague | Ask for the specific step, example, or meaning of the term. |
| A proposed feature | Ask what outcome or problem it should address. |
| A broad claim | Ask for the population, period, and supporting evidence. |
| In conflict with another account | Present the difference neutrally and ask who or what can verify it. |
| Outside the stakeholder’s knowledge | Ask who owns the decision or evidence. |
| Uncertain | Record the uncertainty and agree a proportionate way to investigate. |

Avoid bundling unrelated questions or offering your preferred answer within the question. Do not turn every neutral answer into a cross-examination: ask for evidence proportionate to the decision and risk.

## Capture what was discovered

| Question ID | Stakeholder / source | Finding | Type | Evidence | Open point / owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | | | Fact, interpretation, need, rule, assumption, constraint, or question | | |

Do not force every category to contain something. “None identified” is preferable to invented completeness. A hypothetical AI stakeholder is practice material, not real elicitation evidence.

## Ten-minute practice

Select one case and one stakeholder. Ask one opening question, listen, and probe the answer. After ten minutes, summarise the discovered need, applicable rule, and unresolved question if those were uncovered. Compare your coverage with the bank only after the exercise.

## Self-review

- Was each question connected to the decision?
- Did I allow an answer different from my expectation?
- Did I ask the right person and respect their knowledge boundary?
- Did I follow the previous answer rather than read a script?
- Did I seek examples and distinguish evidence from opinion?
- Did I explore at least one relevant exception or competing explanation?
- Did I confirm understanding and agree an owner for open points?

Review actual questions and answers; do not assign yourself a score merely for reading the guide.
